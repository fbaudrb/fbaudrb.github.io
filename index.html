Welcome! I am a master's student in Economics at Seoul National University. My research interests are in labor economics, family economics, applied microeconomics, and microeconometrics.

I study how labor markets and household formation shape earnings inequality, using linked administrative data. I also work with the Health-Family-Labor Economics research team.

Working Paper

High Wage Workers, High Wage Firms, and Spousal Sorting

with Jungmin Lee

Using linked employer-employee and population data from South Korea, we study assortative mating on worker earnings potential and firm wage premiums. Spouses sort positively on both components, even among couples who have never worked at the same firm. A transferable-utility matching model implies that, without firm-related complementarities, the Gini coefficient of predicted household earnings would be 6.2 percent lower.

Earnings Inequality in South Korea: Evidence from Linked Employer-Employee Data

with Jungmin Lee

We use linked employer-employee data and an AKM model to examine changes in earnings inequality in South Korea, focusing on the role of firm wage premium dispersion in the decline after 2020.
